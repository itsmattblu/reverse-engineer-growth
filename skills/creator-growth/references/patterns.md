# Audience patterns

Moves that keep turning up when you take apart how founders and creators grew without ads, each with how to spot it and what it tells you. Check everyone against this list, and name the patterns you find in the report ("Pattern: giveaway behind a comment"), with the evidence. A pattern is a finding, not a judgement.

These come from studying people it worked for. Most people who post the same way don't grow: say so when a pattern could just be luck or timing.

## The engine: what gets attention

### Giveaway behind a comment
"I built a free [tool / template / pack]. Comment [WORD] and I'll send it."
- **Spot it:** a recurring post shape ending in a keyword; comment counts far above likes; the person replying "sent!" to commenters.
- **Means:** each comment is a lead and pushes the post to more people. Can be the person's best format by a wide margin, but not always: measure the group's median, not just the one that went big. Inflates comment counts, so measure it separately.
- **The keyword may be hidden** in the image or after "see more" for logged-out visitors: infer it from the most common one-word comment.

### Free tools as the content
Small free tools (an analyser, a calculator, a checker, a set of AI skills) instead of opinions.
- **Spot it:** posts and pages offering "free" things that do a job; a separate site of free tools; open-source repos.
- **Means:** the tool does the marketing, keeps working after the post fades, and shows what the paid product will be like. Free tools that rank in search also bring visitors long after.

### The best giveaway becomes the product
The free tool that got the most interest is built out into the paid product.
- **Spot it:** the product does what an earlier giveaway did, bigger; the product name or keyword appeared first as a comment keyword.
- **Means:** demand was tested in public before building. The people who asked for the giveaway are the first users.

### Build it live, with a number
"I built [thing] in [N hours]", then daily updates with users or revenue.
- **Spot it:** a run of posts about one build, each with a number; a launch inside the run.
- **Means:** people follow the story and want to try what they watched being made. Spikes fade unless the product keeps people.

### Milestones with numbers
"$0 to $[X] in [N] days", "[N] users in 24 hours", revenue screenshots.
- **Spot it:** numbers first, short story after; screenshots of dashboards.
- **Means:** proof and curiosity in one line. Numbers are claims unless verified.

### A big total with a failure arc
"[N] products, most failed, here's what I learned."
- **Spot it:** posts counting failures alongside wins.
- **Means:** honesty about failure travels as well as wins, and makes the wins believable.

### Reach from off-topic posts
Their biggest posts are life, jokes, hot takes or news, not the product.
- **Spot it:** the top posts by raw reach have nothing to do with what they sell.
- **Means:** these grow the follower count; product posts then convert a slice. Copying only the product posts misses half the engine.

### Explaining the news first
"[Event] happened. I read all of it. [N] things I learned."
- **Spot it:** a breakout on the day of a big announcement in their field.
- **Means:** being first and clear on a story everyone cares about borrows its attention.

### Riding a live argument
Shipping a product or post that answers a debate happening right now.
- **Spot it:** a launch that replies to, quotes or names a public argument.
- **Means:** the argument brings the audience; the product is the punchline.

### Funny proof from users
"People are using my app for [unexpected thing]."
- **Spot it:** screenshots of users doing something odd with the product.
- **Means:** shows the product in use without a pitch.

## How it's set up

### The founder is the channel
A person's profile, not the brand's, does the posting.
- **Spot it:** the brand account is small or silent; the founder's profile links the product.
- **Means:** people follow people. The founder's earlier audience is often what launched the product.

### One platform first
Most of the growth came from one platform; the others were added later or copy across.
- **Spot it:** one account much bigger and more active than the rest.
- **Means:** depth on one platform beat presence on all of them. Find which one and why it suited their buyers.

### Years before the spike
A long stretch of near-daily posting before the launch that "went viral".
- **Spot it:** the account's age and post count against the launch date.
- **Means:** the overnight success had a year or more behind it. Plan for that.

### The reply shortlist
Growing by replying early and well to bigger accounts in the niche.
- **Spot it:** a post count dominated by replies; follows far more accounts than average; early growth before their own posts did well.
- **Means:** replies are how small accounts get seen. Works when the replies add something; generic ones get muted.

### Revenue as a scoreboard
Each product's revenue listed in the bio.
- **Spot it:** "$[X]/m" figures in the bio, updated over time.
- **Means:** every reply they leave advertises their results. Some stop sharing once copycats arrive.

### Ship many, post every ship
Lots of small products, each launched in public.
- **Spot it:** a list of products, many dead; a launch post for each.
- **Means:** every launch is content even when the product fails, and the audience grows each time.

### The language switch
Moving from their own language to English (or the reverse) for a bigger market.
- **Spot it:** posts change language at a date; the monthly median engagement before and after.
- **Means:** a bigger market can mean a smaller, colder audience at first. Measure the before and after.

### Paid clippers
Paying other people per view to post clips of their videos, instead of running every platform themselves.
- **Spot it:** many accounts on TikTok, Reels or Shorts posting their clips, no account of their own there; a clipping programme or site linked from their bio ("$X per 1,000 views").
- **Means:** reach on platforms they never post to, at a price per view; the clips point back to their main channel or product.

### A burst across platforms
When something starts working on one platform, posting much more everywhere at once for a few months.
- **Spot it:** the posting rate on two or three platforms jumps in the same months as a breakout on one.
- **Means:** they push a winning moment hard while attention is high; the turning point on one platform pulls the others up.

### Repurposing one piece everywhere
One piece of writing becomes a post, a newsletter, a video and a podcast.
- **Spot it:** the same idea on several platforms within days.
- **Means:** more reach for the same work; the native version on each platform does better than a pasted copy.

## Turning followers into users

### Pre-sale through calls
Selling before or right after building, one conversation at a time.
- **Spot it:** posts about calls booked, a pre-sale count, "DM me", a booking link; sign-up asking for a phone number.
- **Means:** early money and learning come from talking to people, not from the post's reach.

### Free product, paid plan later
The product is free at launch; a paid plan is "coming".
- **Spot it:** "100% free", a pricing page with no checkout, terms saying the paid offer isn't on sale.
- **Means:** they're buying users with free; revenue isn't proven yet.

### Story launch
Launch day is a story post (the struggle, the team, what it does, "it's free", the link), often with video, sent to an audience warmed up by weeks of building in public.
- **Spot it:** launch posts by every founder on the same day; build-in-public posts in the weeks before.
- **Means:** the launch works because of the weeks before it.

### The search hand-off
Posting launches the product; search, word of mouth or partners keep it growing.
- **Spot it:** the founder's own traffic breakdowns; programmatic pages; affiliate programmes; comparison pages.
- **Means:** an audience gives a spike; something else keeps it going. Check what that is before copying only the posting.

## Audience and buyers

### The audience is the market
They sell to people like their followers (founders selling to founders, creators to creators).
- **Spot it:** commenters' roles match the product's buyers.
- **Means:** posting turns into customers directly. The strongest cases look like this.

### A big audience of the wrong people
Lots of followers who don't need the product.
- **Spot it:** commenters are other builders, creators or far-off accounts, not the product's buyers; follower count far ahead of revenue.
- **Means:** reach without sales. A smaller audience of buyers would be worth more.

## Inflation and copies

### Engagement pods or bought comments
Groups or services that comment on each other's posts.
- **Spot it:** many polished, generic comments soon after posting, from accounts in countries the product doesn't serve, often with "automation" or "growth" in their headlines.
- **Means:** comment counts overstate real interest. Weigh the post by who commented, not how many.

### Self-reviews and bursts
- **Spot it:** the founder reviewing their own product; several 5-star reviews on one day before launch.
- **Means:** early ratings say little.

### Copied viral posts
- **Spot it:** a breakout's opening line, searched in quotes, appears first from someone else days earlier.
- **Means:** some reach is borrowed. Copy the format, never the post. If you can't tell which came first, say "likely adapted" and name both with their dates.

### Going quiet on numbers
Stopping sharing revenue or details after a point.
- **Spot it:** monthly revenue posts that stop; "we no longer share numbers".
- **Means:** copycats, envy or safety. The last shared number is not today's.
