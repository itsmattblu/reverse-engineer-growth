# Post formats, platform rules and voice

Formats that keep turning up in the posts of founders who grew without ads, how to write each, and what each platform rewards. They come from people it worked for; most posts in any format do ordinary numbers. Measure before believing a format works for this person.

## Formats

### Build story
What they made or changed this week, and why. Shape: the change in one line, the reason or the problem it solves, one detail only they'd know, what's next.
- Strong when it carries a decision ("I cut the paid plan, here's why") or a surprise.
- Weak when it's a changelog line ("Shipped dark mode").

### Output
Something their product or work produced that's useful on its own: a teardown of a known brand, a finding from data, a before and after. Shape: the finding first, three to five specifics, how they found it (the product, lightly), what the reader can take from it.
- The best way to show a product without pitching it. Public brands only, facts anyone can check.

### Giveaway behind a comment
"I made [a free tool / template / skill / checklist]. Comment [WORD] and I'll send it." Shape: what it does for the reader in one line, what's in it (3–4 points), the keyword.
- Only for something real and useful in seconds. The person sends it by hand (a DM or a reply with the link): never automate that.
- Comment counts inflate on purpose; judge it by how many people use the thing.
- Often a person's best format, but the median giveaway does far less than the best one: measure the group.

### Lesson with a number
What they measured, tried or got wrong, with the number. Shape: the number or the mistake first, what happened, what they'd do now.
- "Took 45 minutes instead of 20" beats "Things take longer than you think".

### Milestone
"$0 to [X] in [N] days", "[N] users", "first paying customer". Numbers first, the story after. Only true, sourced numbers; say what they don't know yet.

### Opinion
A clear view on their field, backed by something they saw. Shape: the view in one line, the evidence, the honest limit of it.
- Disagreement is fine; contempt and rage-bait get muted and reported, which hurts reach on X more than likes help.

### Explaining the news
A big announcement in their field, read properly, explained first. Shape: what happened, the 3–5 things that matter, what it means for the reader.
- Works only on the day or the day after.

### Story or personal
Something from their life or work that isn't about the product. Many founders' biggest posts are these; they grow the following that product posts later reach. Optional: only what they want to share.

### Experiment
Any format above in a new shape (a carousel, a video, a thread, a different time, a different length), labelled so the result can be compared with their usual.

## LinkedIn

- **The first two lines** show before "see more" (about 210 characters on desktop, less on phones). Put the point or the tension there.
- **Length:** 100–250 words for most posts; short paragraphs of one or two sentences.
- **Links:** in the first comment, not the post.
- **Carousels (PDF documents)** had by far the highest median engagement in a 2026 study of LinkedIn formats; suggest one when the content is a list or steps.
- **Demoted since 2026:** engagement bait ("comment YES"), videos unrelated to their text, recycled thought-leadership. A giveaway keyword is fine when the thing is real.
- **Replies:** answering comments in the first hour helps the post.
- **Scheduling:** LinkedIn's own scheduler is free.

## X

- **Length:** under 280 characters for most posts; a thread of 3–6 only when the idea needs steps. 100–200 characters did best in one large study.
- **Links:** in a reply. Posts with links from accounts without Premium get almost no engagement.
- **What X ranks:** replies and shares count several times a like; mutes and reports count heavily against. Posts that start a conversation travel.
- **Show, don't tell:** a screenshot or short screen recording of the thing working.
- **Hashtags** add nothing.
- **Replies to others** grow small accounts more than posts do; the person writes and sends those themselves.

## Voice

Read voice from their own writing, in this order: posts they liked writing, their notes and messages, their site. Note:
- **Sentence length and rhythm** (short and punchy, or long and careful).
- **Words they use and avoid** (do they say "customers" or "users", "built" or "shipped").
- **Polish** (lowercase and loose, or edited and exact).
- **Humour** (none, dry, self-deprecating).
- **What they never do** (exclamation marks, emoji, hype words, swearing).

Write `parsly/content/voice.md` from it in a few lines, then keep it current from every edit and rejection. Their changes are the best evidence: if they cut a phrase twice, it's a rule.
